<p align="justify">
My research lies at the intersection of <strong>biological imaging</strong>, <strong>fluorescence microscopy</strong>, and <strong>computational image analysis</strong>. I develop methods to visualize and quantify sub-cellular processes with improved spatial and temporal resolution, tackling challenges in big data analysis, 4D visualization, and automated quantification.
</p>

<p align="justify">
Currently, I focus on two main directions: studying the <strong>cell division mechanisms of cyanobacteria</strong> through deep learning-based image analysis, and advancing <strong>quantitative nanoscale imaging of molecular orientation</strong> through polarized super-resolution microscopy.
</p>

***

## <a style="color:red">Cell division of cyanobacteria</a>

#### Deep learning-based algorithms for cell segmentation and tracking

<strong>About:</strong> I develop automatic tracking methods for single cells, filaments, and cell populations of cyanobacteria, combining advanced microscopy with deep learning to study cell division mechanisms and growth dynamics.

## <a style="color:red">Quantitative nanoscale imaging of molecular orientation</a>

#### Polarized super-resolution microscopy for imaging orientational order in biological structures

<strong>About:</strong> Long-standing collaboration with <strong>Sophie Brasselet</strong>'s team at Institut Fresnel, France. We develop polarization-based super-resolution methods to measure the nanoscale organization and 3D orientation of biological filaments such as actin networks.

<strong>Latest:</strong> Our new method <strong>4polar3D</strong>, published in <em>Nature Communications</em> (2026), enables 3D orientation measurements of single molecules in dense actin structures using ratiometric polarization splitting — with minimal optical complexity and fast data processing. <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70852-y" target="_blank">Read the paper →</a>
